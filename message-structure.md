These rules define the expected structure of a complete customer-facing message: introduction, main part and closing. They apply to all supported message languages. Apply the principles according to the purpose and context of the message. Do not invent customer circumstances, prior contacts, errors, facts, solutions, commitments or next steps that are not supported by the available context.

The examples below illustrate the communicative purpose of each message part. They are examples, not fixed templates. Adapt the wording naturally to the message language, customer situation and available context; do not reproduce an example mechanically when it does not fit.

##### ruleId: cpm-kbs-message-structure-general-001

**Instruction:** Structure a complete customer-facing message in three parts and in this order:
1. Introduction
2. Main part
3. Closing

Use meaningful transitions and sentence connections so that the three parts form a coherent whole. Use the introduction and closing to support a positive customer relationship. Use the main part to address the customer's concerns clearly and factually.

##### ruleId: cpm-kbs-message-structure-general-002

**Applies to:** Introduction.  
**Instruction:** Build a positive relationship with the customer in the introduction. Where relevant and supported by the message context, use one or more of the following elements:
- Refer to the previous contact.
- Thank the customer.
- Apologise if Helsana made an error or if an apology is otherwise factually justified by the context.
- Express appreciation, wherever appropriate.
- Show understanding for the customer's situation.

Write the introduction in an appreciative, well-meaning, friendly and personal manner.

**Examples of the intended communicative function (German source examples):**
- Thanking for an enquiry: “Vielen Dank für Ihre Anfrage.”
- Referring to a previous contact: “Wir haben heute zusammen telefoniert. Vielen Dank für das klärende Gespräch und Ihre Zeit.”
- Showing understanding: “Wir verstehen, dass die Situation nicht einfach für Sie ist und Sie uns um Unterstützung bitten.”
- Apologising for a delayed response: “Sie warten schon lange auf eine klärende Nachricht – bitte entschuldigen Sie unsere späte Antwort.”

##### ruleId: cpm-kbs-message-structure-general-003

**Applies to:** Main part.  
**Instruction:** Address the customer's concerns in the main part. Include the elements required by the customer's request and the available context, such as:
- Take up and explicitly address the customer's concern.
- Answer the customer's questions.
- Provide relevant explanations and factual reasoning.
- Highlight added value where applicable.
- Offer alternative solutions where applicable.
- Explain the next steps where applicable.

Use appropriate transitions from the customer's concern to factual or technical information. Write the main part factually, competently, appropriately for the recipient and with a service-oriented focus.

**Examples of the intended communicative function (German source examples):**
- Taking up the concern: “Sie haben Fragen zu unserer Leistungsabrechnung. Gerne erläutern wir Ihnen die einzelnen Positionen.”
- Transitioning to an explanation: “Gerne erläutern wir Ihnen den Sachverhalt:”
- Explaining a decision: “Gerne begründen wir Ihnen, weshalb wir an unserem Entscheid festhalten.”
- Combining explanation and alternatives: “Gerne erläutern wir Ihnen unseren Standpunkt und informieren Sie über andere Möglichkeiten.”

##### ruleId: cpm-kbs-message-structure-general-004

**Applies to:** Closing.  
**Instruction:** End the message in a way that leaves the customer with a positive feeling. Particularly in longer messages, reconnect with the customer rather than ending immediately after factual or technical information. Where relevant and supported by the message context, use one or more of the following elements:
- Express a personal wish.
- Invite the customer to contact Helsana with further questions.
- Thank the customer.
- Apologise if Helsana made an error or if an apology is otherwise factually justified by the context.
- Express appreciation, wherever appropriate.
- Show understanding for the customer's situation.

Write the closing in an appreciative, well-meaning, friendly and personal manner.

**Examples of the intended communicative function (German source examples):**
- Opening the door for questions: “Bei Fragen sind wir gerne für Sie da.”
- Expressing a personal wish: “Wir wünschen Ihnen und Ihrem Mann alles Gute.”
- Reconnecting with the customer: “Wir hoffen, dass dieser Brief die Situation klärt – Ihre Zufriedenheit ist uns sehr wichtig.”

##### ruleId: cpm-kbs-message-structure-general-005

**Instruction:** Do not force every optional relationship-building element into every message. Select elements that are relevant to the customer's situation and avoid unsupported, exaggerated or formulaic statements. In particular, apologise only when the available context justifies an apology, and express understanding, appreciation or personal wishes only when they are appropriate to the known situation.

##### ruleId: cpm-kbs-message-structure-general-006

**Instruction:** Treat example sentences as inspiration, not as reusable templates. Express the intended message naturally in your own words and adapt it to the output language, customer situation and surrounding text. Preserve the meaning and communicative purpose rather than translating or copying a source example word for word.
