These rules cover opening salutations in English customer correspondence. They do not cover closing greetings, address blocks, signatures or marketing-specific forms of address. Preserve supplied names and their spelling; do not infer a person's gender, title, preferred form of address or relationship history. Names in examples are illustrative, not recipient data. Replace bracketed placeholders only with information available in the context.

Where a rule below conflicts with another guideline, the more specific rule wins. In particular, do not extend the long-standing-contact guidance for e-mails to formal letters.

#### ruleId: `cpm-kbs-salutations-en-001`

**Applies to:** Letters.

**Instruction:** Use British English and address the recipient formally in the opening salutation. The guidelines describe letters as formal communication without exceptions.

**Source:** `CPM-KB-SRC-002-en`, PDF page 14, "Formal communication".

#### ruleId: `cpm-kbs-salutations-en-002`

**Applies to:** External correspondence with customers, companies and public authorities.

**Instruction:** Use the recipient's surname when it is known, and spell it correctly. Use the corresponding known form of address.

**Correct when the surname and form of address are known:**

- `Dear Ms Sample`
- `Dear Mr Sample`

**Instruction when the surname is unknown:** Use `Dear Sir or Madam`.

**Correct when the surname is unknown:**

- `Dear Sir or Madam`

**Source:** `CPM-KB-SRC-002-en`, PDF page 14, "Salutation".

#### ruleId: `cpm-kbs-salutations-en-003`

**Instruction:** Use open punctuation: do not put a full stop after a title such as `Mr`, and do not put a comma after the opening salutation.

**Correct:**

- `Dear Mr Sample`
- `Dear Ms Sample`
- `Dear Dr Muster`
- `Dear Sir or Madam`

**Incorrect:**

- `Dear Mr. Sample`
- `Dear Ms Sample,`
- `Dear Dr. Muster,`

**Source:** `CPM-KB-SRC-002-en`, PDF pages 14 and 16, "Formal communication" and "Overview in all languages".

#### ruleId: `cpm-kbs-salutations-en-004`

**Instruction:** Address customers as private individuals, including federal councillors and religious dignitaries. Academic titles such as doctor and professor are the exception. Diplomas do not count as academic titles and must not be included in the salutation.

**Correct example when the doctoral title and surname are known:**

- `Dear Dr Muster`

**Source:** `CPM-KB-SRC-002-en`, PDF pages 15–16, title guidance and "Overview in all languages".

#### ruleId: `cpm-kbs-salutations-en-005`

**Applies to:** E-mails to long-standing contacts with whom a friendly rapport has been established.

**Instruction:** The guidelines permit less formal salutations for long-standing contacts, especially in e-mails. The English opening examples retain `Dear` with `Ms` or `Mr` and the surname.

**Correct examples for e-mails in this context:**

- `Dear Ms Mustermann`
- `Dear Mr Musterli`

**Source:** `CPM-KB-SRC-002-en`, PDF page 15, "Salutation and closing for long-standing contacts".

#### ruleId: `cpm-kbs-salutations-en-006`

**Applies to:** Individual correspondence with a non-binary person who asks for this to be taken into account.

**Instruction:** A person may, for example, be addressed with `Dear [First name] [Surname]`.

**Format:** `Dear [First name] [Surname]`

**Source:** `CPM-KB-SRC-002-en`, PDF page 18, "Salutations for non-binary people".
