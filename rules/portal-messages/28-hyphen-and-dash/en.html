Hyphen and dash

We distinguish between hyphen and dash which are two very different punctuation marks.

Hyphen
A hyphen is short and joins two words together. There’s no need to put any space before or after it.
  • To divide words at the end of a line, but avoid it
  • In compound nouns formed with prepositions and adverb particles: in compound adjectives
  • To tell the ages of people and things

won-der-ful

mother-in-law; make-up
a five-dollar bill (but: five dollars)

a twelve-year-old girl
(but: the girl is twelve years old)

  • To connect separate words and prefixes
  • In fractions and numbers from 21-99 (when written out in full)
  • To list key points

mid-June; pre-meeting
two-thirds of the voters; sixty-five people

- A
- B
- C
Dash
A dash is longer than a hyphen.
  • To set off parenthetical statements as an alternative to commas or brackets; there’s always a space before and after a dash
Ms Taylor – as you know – hasn’t arrived yet.
The influence of the three impressionists – Monet, Sisley and Degas – is obvious in her work.
  • To indicate a range, as an alternative to “to”
World War II (1939–45); pp. 87–101; read pages 5–7
  • To indicate round amounts
CHF 251.–
* How to create a dash (en dash) in Word: press Ctrl and the minus symbol on the numeric keypad (-). In all other programmes: press Alt and type 0150.
