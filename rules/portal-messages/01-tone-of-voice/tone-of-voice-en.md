## Relevant: we provide answers

### Write punchy messages (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-001`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Write punchy messages", p. 2
- **Instruction:** Avoid words that say little, are self-evident or are out of date. Replace general adjectives with concrete information, but only with information from the input. If the input has none, remove the empty adjective or leave the sentence unchanged.
- **Avoid:** `expert advisors` (every advisor is an expert by definition), `good`, `nice`, `accommodating`, `efficient`, `friendly`
- **Not affected:** fixed greetings, closings and wishes, for example `Good afternoon`, `Kind regards`, `With best wishes for a good recovery`.

### Stay simple (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-002`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Stay simple", p. 2; "Be credible", p. 4
- **Instruction:** Concentrate on the essentials. Avoid repetition and words that repeat what another word already says. Do not make wording more complicated than necessary; prefer the plain word.
- **Replace:** `in-depth details` → `details`

### Take the customer's perspective (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-003`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Adopt the perspective of the customer", p. 2–3
- **Instruction:** Avoid the internal view and marketing or business jargon. Drop qualifiers the reader does not need.
- **Avoid:** `added value`, `time window`, `proactive`, `expedient`, `timely`
- **Vague time words** such as `timely`: replace only with a concrete time from the input; otherwise leave them unchanged and report them.
- **Replace:** `insurance consultation` → `consultation`, `insurance coverage` → `coverage`, `insurance benefits` → `benefits`, `customer support team` → `support team`

## Easy to understand: we get to the point

### Keep sentences short (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-004`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Keep sentences as short as possible", p. 3
- **Instruction:** Avoid long and convoluted sentences and strings of nouns. Use at most one subordinate clause per sentence. In longer sentences, put the key verb as early as possible.

### Use verbs, not nominalisations (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-005`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Be clear", p. 3–4
- **Instruction:** Leave nominalisations to legal texts. Use the verb instead, because it shows more clearly who does what.

### Explain technical terms (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-007`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Relevant: we provide answers", p. 2
- **Instruction:** Use simple language. Explain any technical term the reader may not know, in the text itself. Use only an explanation from the input or an approved glossary; when reviewing, report an unexplained term instead of writing a definition. For abbreviations, see section 03, Abbreviations.

### Address the reader directly (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-008`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Address people directly", p. 4
- **Instruction:** Put the reader at the centre. As a rule, do not refer to Helsana by name; use `we` instead (see also section 12, Helsana the brand).
- **Not affected:** the signature, product and service names (for example `Helsana+`, the Helsana app) and the names of legal entities.

## Transparent: we write the way we are

### Be realistic: no superlatives (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-009`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Be realistic", p. 5
- **Instruction:** Avoid meaningless superlatives and exaggeration. Prefer understatement and let the service speak for itself.
- **Avoid:** `extraordinary`, `first-rate`, `excellent`, `fantastic`, `the best`, `the most`
- **Not affected:** fixed greetings and closings, for example `Best regards`, `Best wishes`.

### Be accessible: no relativising words (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-010`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Be accessible", p. 5
- **Instruction:** Avoid modal verbs and adverbs that weaken a statement and create distance.
- **Report:** `actually`, `arguably` and `like` used as filler, and `can`, `should` when the sentence stays true without them (`you can actually benefit from a wide range of services` → `you benefit from a wide range of services`).
- **Not affected:** modal verbs that state a real option, permission or obligation (`You can cancel by 30 November`, `You must submit the form`), and words such as `probably` that express real uncertainty about a fact. Removing them would turn an uncertain statement into a promise.

### Write in the active voice (`cpm-kbs-tone-en-011`)

- **Severity:** guideline
- **Source:** Corporate language EN, "Write in the active voice", p. 6
- **Instruction:** Write in the active voice. Passive sentences are clumsy and make actions impersonal. Name the actor only if the input makes it clear; otherwise keep the passive.
